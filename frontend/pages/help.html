Guidance for every part of the system. Search filters the sections as you type.

Getting started — the everyday flow
  1. Master data first. Add customers, suppliers, raw materials and products before recording anything.
  2. Buy stock. Purchase invoices record what you owe suppliers; supplier payments reduce it.
  3. Produce. Production entries consume raw material and create finished-goods stock.
  4. Sell. A challan is created as a draft. Confirming it deducts finished stock and raises the customer's dues. Dispatch, then mark delivered.
  5. Collect money. Payments against a challan reduce its balance and the customer's outstanding.

A challan only becomes a receivable when it is confirmed. Drafts are safe to experiment with and can be deleted outright.

Product categories — what they are for

A category is a label shared by products, picked from a fixed master list (Products → Categories) instead of typed freely, so every product groups the same way everywhere.

  1. Assign it where the product is created. The product form's Category dropdown lists the master list; choose + New category the first time you need one.
  2. Filter the product list. The All categories dropdown above the product table shows only that category, and the search box also matches category names.
  3. Group reports. Stock Analysis groups figures by category, and the category table shows how many products sit in each.

Manage the list itself (add, rename, deactivate, delete) in the Categories card. Deactivated categories disappear from pickers but keep their existing products grouped under them.

Payroll

Payroll is on its own page. The monthly run is deliberately two steps: choose a month, press Preview month, read the figures, then press Run payroll. Nothing is written until you press Run, so a salary entered wrongly can be spotted before everybody has been paid.

How net pay is worked out

net pay = salary + allowances − deductions − advance recovered

You do not type the net pay. The app calculates it and will refuse a figure that disagrees with the formula, so the register can never hold a total which does not match its own inputs.

Advances are recovered automatically, oldest first, whenever a run happens. Recovery is capped so net pay can never go below zero: if someone owes more in advance than the month's pay can cover, the remainder carries forward and the preview says so rather than producing a negative paycheque.

A month can only be paid once. Anyone already paid for that month is shown as Paid and skipped, so pressing Run twice cannot pay anyone twice. This is enforced by the database, not just by the screen.

Attendance is recorded per person per day, and re-marking a day corrects it rather than adding a second row. Attendance is shown alongside each employee in the preview for information; it does not change the amount paid under this formula.

Leave is requested per person and needs approving or rejecting. Days are counted for you from the dates given.

Deductions are recorded against a month. A run marks them as applied instead of deleting them, so the register still explains where a figure went.

Only a Manager or Admin can run payroll or record advances and deductions — this is separate from ordinary employee editing, which data entry can do.

On a shared computer, use Switch User in the top bar when handing over — signing in as yourself keeps the audit history honest about who did what. An idle session also signs itself out after the minutes set in Settings › Session Lock (0 disables).

Challan vs Sales Tax Invoice

These are two different documents and the difference matters for tax.

Delivery Challan Sales Tax Invoice
Proves the goods left The final bill, with GST
Not a tax document Needed for your customer to claim input credit
No GST shown Taxable value, GST rate, CGST/SGST or IGST

To create a sales invoice, open a challan and use the Sales Invoice… button next to Print Challan. It becomes Print Sales Invoice once one exists. A challan can only ever be invoiced once, so you cannot double-bill the same delivery.

The WhatsApp ↗ button beside it opens WhatsApp with the invoice summary prefilled — total and balance due — addressed to the customer's phone number. Sending, and attaching the PDF, stay manual; if the customer has no usable number the button says so instead. The PDF button downloads the same invoice as a file, ready to attach — with the company logo and Hindi names intact. The Email PDF button mails that file: the address box opens prefilled with the customer email on file, so leaving it blank sends it there; typing an address overrides it for that send only.

The total does not change. GST is taken out of the challan amount rather than added on top. A challan of 1150.00 at 18% becomes 1000.00 of goods plus 150.00 of tax — the sales invoice still totals 1150.00, so your customer is never re-billed.

Before you can invoice, two things must be set (the Settings page lists anything still missing):

  • Each product needs a GST rate on the Products page. Until one is set, a sales invoice is refused rather than issued with no tax — and no product is defaulted to a guess.
  • Your company GSTIN must be in the Company Profile. A tax invoice without it is not valid, so one will not be issued.

An HSN code per product is optional here but worth filling in — your customer's accountant may ask for it. Typing the product name suggests the code automatically; anything you pick or type yourself always wins.

Payments stay on the challan, not the sales invoice. That is deliberate: one place tracks what you are owed, so a receivable cannot be counted twice. The sales invoice shows the challan's balance read-only.

Intra- or inter-state? This factory is registered in Bihar, so a customer in another state needs the supply type set to Inter-state in Settings — those invoices are charged IGST instead of CGST + SGST. Archive, Restore and Delete

There are two ways to remove a record, and they are not interchangeable.

Archive — the normal way

  • Hides the record from lists, but keeps its effect on stock, balances and reports.
  • Fully reversible — you can bring it back at any time.
  • Archived rows are dimmed and badged, so you can see them once you tick Show archived.

Archived records are read-only

While a record is archived you cannot edit it, dispatch it, change its status or take a payment against it. This is deliberate: the archive exists to preserve a record exactly as it was. You will see "This challan is archived. Restore it before making changes." if you try.

Restore

Tick Show archived, then press Restore on the row. The record returns to the normal list and becomes editable again.

Restoring a payment also un-archives its parent challan or invoice, because a payment is only reachable through its parent. The app tells you when it does this. The reverse is not true: restoring a challan does not restore a separately archived payment.

Delete — only for an unpaid draft

The Delete button appears only on draft challans that have never been confirmed and have no payment. This is a permanent delete and cannot be undone, though the deletion itself is written to the audit history.

Once a challan is confirmed, stock and the customer's dues have already been posted, so deletion is refused and you should archive instead. The app explains which condition blocked it.

Why archiving does not change totals

Archiving never reverses money or stock, so archived documents keep counting in reports and balances. That is what makes it safe — and it is why nothing needs recalculating when you restore.

Why am I asked for a reason?

Every edit, archive, restore and status change asks for a short note (at least 5 characters) before it is saved. The note is stored in the audit history beside who made the change and when, so months later "why was this rate changed?" has a real answer.

Normal work never asks: creating a challan, invoice, payment or expense needs no note. Imports don't ask either. The question appears only when something already recorded is being changed.

Tip: press Ctrl+Enter inside the note box to submit it. Cancelling the note box cancels the change — nothing is saved without a reason.

Products and pack sizes (weights)

A product is sold in several pack sizes. The product is the thing you sell (for example Shresth Chilli Masala); each pack size is a weight of it (175g, 200g, 250g, 500g).

Adding another weight to an existing product

Type the same product name and the new weight. The app recognises the name and attaches the weight to the existing product instead of creating a duplicate. It then tells you which sizes were added and which were already present.

You can also press Edit on a product and use Add pack size in the panel that opens.

Prices are per pack size

Each size carries its own rate, because price per kg differs by pack. The Rate column on the products list shows one rate per size, aligned with the sizes beside it.

Which rate is used

When you add a challan line, the price is taken in this order:

  1. the rate typed on that line, if you entered one
  2. otherwise the rate of that pack size
  3. otherwise the product's fallback rate

A fallback rate is a safety net, not a normal price. It is only used for a pack size left with no rate of its own, and the app warns you by name whenever that happens — in the product form, after saving a rate, and as a standing note in the Edit panel.

Formulation — the mix ratio

A mix masala can keep its formulation: each raw material with its share of the mix, totalling exactly 100%. Edit the product to add ingredients; the editor totals the shares live and shows the indicative cost per kg at today's purchase rates. The ratio is a mixing reference — it never moves stock — and clearing it is just saving an empty list.

Below the ratio, processing steps record how the mix is made, in order — free text like "Roast at 120°C for 20 min, then cool", each optionally tagged with its stage (roasting, grinding, mixing, packaging). When that product is picked on a new production entry, the ratio and steps show as guidance above the form; Apply formulation… fills the materials from the ratio for the target qty.

Every product also has its own Help button: an independent blank window for the formulation and process in plain words. It is shown while producing, alongside the ratio and steps above.

Category

Category comes from the fixed master list (Products › Categories), not typed freely — that is what keeps every product grouped the same way in reports. It affects no price, stock figure or report total; it is there so you can filter Stock Analysis by it and find products by name in the product list.

Removing a pack size

Press Edit on the product, then Remove beside the size. This retires it rather than deleting it: past challans and stock entries keep reading it, but it is no longer offered for anything new. The last size of a product cannot be removed — add the replacement first.

Typing the same name again later restores the retired size instead of creating a duplicate.

Low-stock threshold per size

Each pack size carries its own threshold in packets, set in the same Edit panel. Fall below it and the size joins the low-stock alerts on the dashboard, the Alerts page and the daily report mail. Zero (the default) only alerts when the size is fully out; retired sizes never alert.

Changing a rate

Press Edit on the product. Every pack size gets its own row with a Rate/kg box and a Save button, so you can change one weight without touching the others. The History button beside it shows every past rate with who changed it and when — useful when margins move and nobody remembers why.

  • Rates can be changed at any time, and each stock movement keeps the rate it was valued at.
  • Weights lock once a size has stock history. Changing a weight would silently restate historical stock, so the app refuses it and tells you the rate can still be changed.
  • Renaming a product onto another product's name is refused, and a weight in the product name (like "Shresth Chilli 5kg") is refused — put weights in packaging sizes instead.
Sales challans
  • Draft — being prepared. No stock or money effect. Safe to delete.
  • Confirmed — stock deducted, customer owes the amount. Cannot be deleted.
  • Partially paid / Paid — payments recorded against it.
  • Closed — finished; no further changes.

Use Dispatch to record the transporter, vehicle and date, then Mark Delivered when it arrives. A closed challan cannot be dispatched.

Payment is refused on a draft — confirm it first, because a draft is not yet a receivable.

A customer can carry a credit limit (set on the customer form, 0 = unlimited). Confirming a challan that would push their outstanding past it is refused with the shortfall — switch Editor Mode on to override, which is written to the audit history.

The Balance Due column is always total minus paid, and the customer's outstanding is the sum of their live challan balances plus any opening balance.

Printing a challan for the customer

Open any challan and press 🖨 Print Challan. You get an A4 document with your letterhead, the customer's details, every product with its pack size, quantity, weight, rate and amount, the total written in words, space to sign, and your terms at the foot. Press Print in the browser dialog, or choose Save as PDF there to get a PDF file instead.

Your company name, address, GSTIN, phone, email and terms come from Settings › Company Profile. Anything left blank there simply does not print.

Why an old challan never changes

Each challan keeps its own private copy of your company details, the customer's details, and the product and pack-size name on every line. Editing any of those later does not alter a challan that already exists — a document somebody is holding stays exactly as it was issued. If a printed challan has a genuine mistake in it, archive it and raise a corrected one.

The one-time exception: the first time you save a company profile, challans created before then are filled in with those details so they do not print blank. Later saves leave them alone.

A challan with no recorded company details says so on the printout rather than showing an empty header, so a missing letterhead is never mistaken for a bug.

Purchases, suppliers and payments
  • A purchase invoice records goods received and what you owe the supplier.
  • Transport Charges (freight) and Other Charges are your costing only: they never change what you owe the supplier. Each line shows its share of the charges in ₹ (weight × ₹/kg — a 10 kg line and a 20 kg line split ₹100 as ₹33.33 and ₹66.67) plus Total Cost ₹/kg (rate + GST + the charge share), which is the same for every kg of the invoice.
  • The Total follows the goods value automatically, but you can type over it to follow the paper invoice — dues then key off your figure.
  • A supplier payment reduces the purchase-invoice balance and the supplier's outstanding. It cannot exceed the invoice's outstanding.
  • Supplier outstanding = opening balance + all purchase-invoice totals − all payments.

Payments and invoices archive and restore like any other record.

Production, raw material and finished stock
  • A production entry turns raw material into finished goods in a chosen pack size.
  • Target Qty is the plan — how much you intend to make. It moves no stock; the Actual column shows what really came out, as packets and kg (e.g. "200 pkts (50.00 kg)"). If an entry is saved with no output rows at all, the target stands in as the output so the run still records stock.
  • One batch can pack several sizes at once — add a row per size with its packets in the entry form. The batch keeps a single batch number; each size posts its own finished-stock movement from the same raw-material pool.
  • Wrapper (pouch) weight is tracked per size: set each pouch's empty weight in the product editor, then pick the wrapper roll on the production form. The quantity fills itself (packets × pouch weight) and posts as ordinary raw-material consumption. Wrapper rolls are bought and stocked like any raw material in kg.
  • Apply formulation… on the production form fills the consumed materials from the product's saved mix ratio, scaled to the Target Qty (50 kg at 40% → 20 kg) — replacing the rows already there. Rows stay editable afterwards, and the message states the scaled material cost.
  • Status moves draft → in progress → completed, or cancelled. It cannot go back to draft.
  • Each entry has stages (Roasting, Grinding, Mixing, Packaging) where you record input, output and wastage.
  • Stage loss is worked out for you from the input and output you enter: loss = input − output, shown as a percentage of the input. You record the two real numbers and the percentage follows, so the two can never disagree.
  • A stage that reports more output than input is treated as zero loss rather than a negative figure — on roasting, grinding and mixing that combination is a data mistake, and it will not quietly reduce your totals. The packaging stage is the exception (see Packing gain below).

Two different kinds of loss

Stage loss is material lost while processing (roasting, grinding, mixing, packaging). It is recorded against the production entry and appears in the Category Summary on the Reports page.

Finished-goods write-off is packets removed from finished stock after they were made — damaged, expired or broken. It is a stock movement in its own right and shows on the Stock Analysis page, not in the Category Summary.

Stock Adjustments for finished goods are typed in packets by default — pick the size, type the count, and the "= kg" line shows the conversion using that size's own weight. The kg toggle is there for torn or loose stock. The movement history shows both figures ("10 pkt = 1 kg").

Wastage is reported as a share of the total material put into production, not as a share of final output. Loss compounds down a multi-stage workflow, so dividing by final output would overstate the rate considerably.

Packing gain (packaging only)

Packed output can legitimately weigh more than the loose masala that went in, because the pouches themselves add weight. When a packaging stage's output exceeds its input, the surplus is stored as packing gain — with zero wastage on that stage, and shown in its own Gain column on the entry and in the Category Summary. It is wrapper weight, not negative wastage, so it is never subtracted from loss totals. On the form the packaging row announces it ("Packing gain +X kg (wrapper)") as you type.

Stock checks

Stock Analysis reconciles raw and finished stock against their movement ledgers, and highlights wastage. If a figure looks wrong, run it — it will tell you whether the ledger or the running balance has drifted.

Raw materials carry a threshold and reorder level; low stock raises an alert on the dashboard.

Expenses
  • Record expenses against a category (Raw Material, Packaging, Salary & Wages, Transport, Utilities, Maintenance).
  • Expenses are filtered by the same search box and date range as everything else.
  • Archiving an expense hides it but keeps it in expense totals, for the audit trail.
Dashboard, Recent Transactions and global search

Recent Transactions

The dashboard list shows the newest records first across challans, purchases, payments and expenses. Expense rows show the expense description as their reference — that is the expense's name — with the payee beside it.

Global search (top bar)

Search covers customers, challans, purchase invoices, products, suppliers, payments and expenses. Receipts and payments are found by their RCPT-/PAY- code or party name; expenses by description, category or payee.

Opening a payment result jumps straight to that receipt or payment: the parent record opens with the exact row highlighted. An expense result filters the Expenses page to it — when several expenses share one description (monthly rent), each hit shows its date, amount and category so you pick the right month before opening.

Reports, filters and the date range

"Current FY only"

The financial year runs 1 April to 31 March. Ticking Current FY only sets the From date to the FY start and filters to this year.

The checkbox is entirely yours: it changes only when you click it. Setting a custom From/To date no longer switches it off, and your choice is remembered per page — including after navigating away and coming back.

Filters remember themselves

Date ranges and the "Show archived" toggle persist per page. Clear filter on the Reports page resets them deliberately.

Refreshing

The ⟳ Refresh button in the top bar reloads the current page's data while keeping your filters. On pages that own their own data-loading routine it refreshes in place instead of reloading the page.

Other reports

  • Sales Trend — sales over time.
  • Monthly Sales — a month-by-month pivot with year fill-in.
  • Custom Reports — save your own column picks and filters, then run them from the sidebar.
Audit history

Every create, update, archive, restore, delete, status change, dispatch, delivery, payment, stock movement and rate change is recorded with who did it and when.

  • Filter by module and action to narrow things down.
  • Press View on any row to see the Before and After values in a popup, with the user, timestamp and IP address.
  • Permanent deletes are recorded too — the audit row is written before the record is removed, so the deletion never vanishes from the trail.

Requires the Audit view permission (Admin and Manager by default). It can be granted to other roles from the Users page.

Roles and permissions
Role Can do
AdminEverything, plus Users, Backup and Settings.
ManagerAll day-to-day work: create, edit, confirm, dispatch, collect, archive, restore. No user or system admin.
Data EntryCreate and edit master data and documents. Cannot delete, approve, confirm, dispatch or collect payments.
ViewerRead-only. All write buttons are hidden and the server refuses writes.
Challan EntryChallans only: can view the challan list and create challans (with customer/product pickers). No payments, reports, stock, payroll, settings or user admin. For counter staff who only raise delivery notes.

Some abilities are fixed role floors and cannot be granted away — for example a Viewer can never write, and Data Entry can never delete. Everything else is an individual permission (such as Collect payment, Confirm challan, Restore record) that an Admin can tick per role on the Users page.

If a button is missing, your role lacks the permission behind it. If a message says "Manager access required" or "Permission required: …", that is the same thing seen from the server.

Import and Export
  • Import masters and transactions from Excel. Download a template first so the columns match. Import in dependency order: Customers → Suppliers → Raw Materials → Products → Purchase Invoices → Sales Challans → Customer Payments → Expenses → Employees.
  • Customer Payments records receipts against challans already in the system — one row per payment with its own date. Draft, archived, unknown-challan and over-outstanding rows are skipped with the reason.
  • The preview shows what will be created or updated before anything is written, and reports per-row failures afterwards.
  • Export pulls data out as a spreadsheet for Excel or accounting.

Two formats, one set of figures. Every export offers Excel and PDF. Both are produced from the same query, so a PDF and its matching spreadsheet can never disagree — which is the point, because a report whose two versions differ is worse than either being wrong on its own.

The PDF is a real file you can email or keep, not a print dialog. It repeats its column headings on every page and numbers its pages, so a printed pack can be checked for missing sheets. Filtering to a range with no matching rows still produces a valid PDF that says so, rather than an error or an empty file.

On the Reports page, a PDF of a custom report records the source, the date range and the column selection on the document itself — so it is obvious later which period it covered.

Bulk import needs the Bulk import permission (Admin and Manager by default).

Backup and restore
  • Backup Now + Save As (Admin only) copies the whole database, then asks where to keep that copy — e.g. a pen drive. The History below always keeps the in-app copy too.
  • Backup folder in Settings › Backups changes where copies are stored (empty = default). Times are shown in IST (UTC+05:30).
  • Download saves a copy of a backup to your computer. Download + Key saves a ZIP containing the backup and its secret key file — the key signs sign-in tokens, so keep the two together if you ever need the copy on another machine.
  • Delete removes a backup and its key file. Manual backups are never auto-deleted.
  • Automatic backups run daily while the app is running; the sidebar footer shows the most recent one. The hour and how many auto copies to keep are set in Settings › Automatic Backups.

Restore

Pressing Restore asks where the copy should go: the live database (replaces current data) or a custom path (a harmless copy for inspection, current data untouched).

  • Before a live restore, the current database is automatically saved as a safety copy, so a wrong choice can be undone.
  • After a live restore, restart the app before doing anything else.

The history lists only backups the app itself created. Copying a database file into data/backups by hand does not add it to the list.

Product categories

Every product belongs to one category, and the category comes from a fixed list rather than being typed in. That is what makes the reports add up: free text quietly turns into Dry Masala, dry masala and DRY MASALA, and each one reports as a separate business line.

Managing the list

  • The Categories card on the Products page lists them all, with how many products sit in each.
  • Add a category there, or from the + New category option inside the Add Product form and the product editor.
  • Rename changes the name everywhere at once — products, reports and the Analysis page all follow.
  • Deactivate hides a category from new pickers but keeps it on the products that already use it, so history stays intact.
  • Delete only works on an unused category. To retire one that products still use, deactivate it instead.

Nesting

A category can sit under another using the Under picker. The reports show the parent in brackets, and a category with sub-categories cannot be deleted out from under them. Sub-grouping is a display convenience — totals are still counted per category.

Reporting

The Category Summary on the Reports page shows, for each category: revenue and its share of the total, quantity sold, total material processed, final output, finished stock in kg and at value, and stage loss. It answers which part of the business earns the most, which one is sitting on the most stock, and where the processing loss is concentrated.

Sales count only live, non-draft challans. Stock value is the finished-goods balance from the stock ledger valued at each pack size's own rate. Stage loss is the material lost at each production stage, as a share of the total processed. Finished-goods write-offs are not included — see Production, raw material and finished stock. A product with no category is still counted, under (uncategorised), so nothing quietly disappears.

Finding products by category

The All categories dropdown above the product list narrows the table to one category, and combines with the search box.

Editor Mode

Editor Mode is a safety switch that must be turned on before anything in the system can be changed or removed.

It is off when you sign in

Every fresh sign-in starts with Editor Mode off, so you can never arrive already able to delete things. Look for the padlock button in the top bar: 🔒 Editor Mode: OFF.

Two separate checks

Holding edit or delete permission is not enough on its own. Both must be true:

  1. your role has the permission, and
  2. you have switched Editor Mode on yourself.

Switching it on grants nothing extra — it only removes the second lock. Role rules still apply exactly as before.

What it covers

Every change and every removal: editing a record, archiving, restoring, deleting, closing a challan, changing a status, editing a price, and changing users, permissions, settings or backups.

Two harmless things stay available so you are not forced into Editor Mode to tidy up: your dashboard layout and clearing the notification list. Saving the company profile and revoking a dashboard share link are also exempt — the first is admin-only configuration with no issued document at risk, the second only takes a link off the internet.

Reading and viewing are never affected

You can always browse, search, filter, view details and run reports with Editor Mode off. Only changes are blocked.

It is recorded

Switching Editor Mode on or off is written to the audit history, so you can see when it was used and by whom.

"Editor Mode is off. Switch Editor Mode on…"

You tried to change something while it was off. Use the padlock button in the top bar, then try again.

While it is off, the destructive and approval buttons on each row are dimmed. Pressing one does not change anything — instead a popup explains exactly why it is unavailable (Editor Mode off, or the record's state, such as a draft challan that must be confirmed before payment).

Troubleshooting

"This challan is archived. Restore it before making changes."

You are trying to change an archived record. Tick Show archived, press Restore, then do your change.

"Insufficient finished stock to confirm"

Confirming a challan needs the stock to actually exist. Check Finished stock for that product and pack size, record the production run or an opening adjustment that supplies it, then confirm.

A challan will not delete

Only unpaid drafts can be deleted. If it is confirmed, it has posted stock and a receivable — archive it instead. The error message names the exact reason.

"Material ID already exists"

Raw material codes must be unique. Reuse the existing material rather than creating a second one.

A weight will not change

That pack size already has stock history, so its weight is locked to protect historical stock. Change the rate instead, or add a new pack size.

A button is missing

Your role lacks the permission. See Roles and permissions; an Admin can grant it on the Users page.

A figure looks wrong

Open Stock Analysis first — it reconciles stock against the movement ledger and names the drift. Challan, invoice and party balances are always derivable as total minus paid; the audit history shows who changed what.

Numbers in the list look stale

Press ⟳ Refresh in the top bar. It reloads the current page's data and keeps your filters.
